Privacy

SakuraOS does not collect information about you or your computer. There is no account and no telemetry. Your computer does contact some servers on its own, mainly to check for updates; this page lists every one of them.

What SakuraOS collects

Nothing. No usage statistics, no crash reports, no hardware details and no identifier for your computer are sent to SakuraOS.

The only telemetry on the system belongs to KDE, which makes the Plasma desktop. It sends anonymous information about your hardware and which features you use to KDE, never to SakuraOS, and it is off unless you turned it on in the installer. You can change it in System Settings → User Feedback. KDE's privacy policy says what it collects.

Your web browser, and any application you install, has its own privacy policy. Zen, the default browser, is based on Firefox.

On your local network

Other devices on the same network, such as the people sharing your Wi-Fi, can see your computer's name. The system answers when something looks that name up, and while you are logged in, KDE Connect announces the computer so that your phone can find it. Nothing is shared with a device until you approve it on this computer.

Turning off the connection check

The connection check lets the system notice captive portals, such as hotel Wi-Fi login pages. To turn it off, run the following in a terminal and restart: 

printf '[connectivity]\nenabled=false\n' | sudo tee /etc/NetworkManager/conf.d/20-no-connectivity-check.conf
